01 · About

I support executives, and I automate the routine work around them.

I managed the President's calendar across two campuses and meetings with partners in South Korea and Virginia, arranged international travel for accreditation visits, prepared the agendas and materials for the monthly meetings of the 13-member Board of Directors, took the minutes, and carried the Board's decisions back to university leadership. C · FOR THE DIRECTOR

An application review, checked three ways

At the Director's request, I prepared an institutional application and the President's letter of support for submission. A Claude Code session built a scoring rubric from the program's own documents. A ChatGPT model checked the rubric for gaps and then checked every figure, and fresh Claude sub-agents that had not seen the session's reasoning read the work cold. The Director received Word copies with tracked changes to accept or reject, an audit report with a fix list, and a short email explaining each file.

→before hand-off · I questioned every figure and change

Before anything reached the Director, I questioned the AI's work myself: where each figure came from, why each change was made, and whether the hand-off was short enough for the person who had to act on it.

Also in daily use: a Notion work tracker I built and run through Claude, Trello driven through Claude every day, a script that checks the department's spending records against the District finance system, a Google Form that writes staff absences to the office log automatically, tools that draft press releases, media advisories and invitations in the office's voice, a sourced knowledge base of the office's procedures that answers process questions with citations, and a monthly content-calendar workflow I built for the College Foundation's social media manager, who uses it now.

03 · On my own

What I build in my own time.

The same habits, pointed at my own household and my own work. I direct the AI that writes the code; I decide what it does, test it, and keep what holds up.

Jarvis

A household assistant we text. It reads and edits our calendar, keeps the bills list and trip folders, and drafts email that is sent only after I read it in a text and say yes. A privacy hold in code withholds health, identity, tax, credentials, financial documents and work mail before any text reaches the model. About Jarvis ↗

Review Desk

A Mac app for reviewing AI-made work. I open a video, image or document, mark the spots that need changes, comment on them, and copy the whole batch back to the AI session that made the file.

AI Radar

A scheduled monitor that sweeps AI model releases and news and writes me a daily digest and a weekly synthesis, so I know what changed before I decide which tool to use.

Mindframe

A second brain. Conversations from Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are captured into one inbox, sorted on a weekly pass, and reused as context when I draft.

Local voice models

For my history video channel I recorded my own voice and ran cloning tests on open-source models on my own laptop, with ElevenLabs' cloning as the benchmark.

Testing before trusting

I tested whether an AI could catch passwords my automatic scanners missed, without ever showing it a password. It found gaps, I closed them in the scanners, and after that it had nothing left to add, so I kept the simple rules.

04 · Confidential data

What goes into the AI, and what never does.

I treat an AI prompt like a public forum unless I am working in a secured environment, such as a local model on my own machine.

  • Recordings of in-person meetings are transcribed on my laptop, so the audio does not leave it. Sensitive recordings and transcripts are destroyed as soon as the internal minutes are made; other voice memos and transcripts are deleted on a schedule.
  • Passwords and keys never go into a prompt. They stay in an encrypted password vault, and I rotate any key that is ever exposed.
  • I don't rely on the AI remembering. The important rules in my setup are enforced by scripts that block the action whatever the model does.
  • I follow my employer's AI and student-privacy rules to the letter, and I adapt these habits to each employer's policies.
