Meeting minutes, start to finish.

Minutes are one of the most repeated jobs in executive support. This is the workflow I built for my office: what goes in, what the AI does, what I check, and what gets deleted.

How it works ↓ ← Back to Andrew Park
M1 · The problem

Two to three hours a meeting, by hand.

Drafting minutes by hand typically took two to three hours a meeting, and my office's meetings arrive in different forms.

Digital meetings

Teams and Zoom calls already carry a speaker-labelled transcript. The work is turning it into minutes people can act on.

In-person meetings

Planning sessions, trainings and committee meetings where the only record is a recording.

Names and privacy

Transcripts misspell names, and some recordings hold things that should not sit on a server after the minutes are done.

M2 · How it works

Five steps, one of them mine.

The AI drafts. I check the names, finish the document and send it.

1 · Capture

A Teams or Zoom meeting already produces a speaker-labelled transcript, and digital meetings can also come in through the Fathom meeting recorder. For an in-person meeting, I record it and transcribe the recording on my own laptop.

2 · Draft

A Claude skill I built reads the transcript and writes minutes in the College's branded format, with decisions, action items and the details I want on record.

3 · Check names

The skill flags every name it is unsure of against a list of College names and waits for me to confirm or correct each one. It is a review gate, not an auto-correct.

4 · Finish

I finish the minutes in Word and send them.

5 · Clean up

Sensitive recordings and transcripts are destroyed as soon as the internal minutes are made. Other voice memos and transcripts are deleted on a schedule by an automated job.

M4 · Other workflows in the office

The rest of the routine.

Monthly board report

A Claude skill that builds the report to the Board of Trustees in Canva. A colleague now runs it on his own computer.

Work tracker

A Notion tracker for accounts, agreements, events, travel, leave, tasks and a call log, built and run through Claude, with the District's budget figures for 18 accounts pushed in by script.

Spending check

A script, used alongside my own manual reconciliation, that checks the department's spending records against the District finance system.

Absence log

A Google Form that writes each staff absence to the office's log automatically.

Drafting in the office's voice

Tools that draft press releases, media advisories and invitations from past approved emails, and remarks in an executive's voice.

Foundation content calendar

Skill files, a research document and a living spreadsheet the Foundation's social media manager uses to plan each month's posts.
